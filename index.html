A / 03

Programming

Firmware · API · Captive Portal
Stage 01

Firmware

ESP32-C3 Arduino firmware polling Open-Meteo for rain probability on a configurable schedule and translating the forecast into colour and animation on a WS2811 strip. Sensitivity threshold, colour presets, and strip length are all user-tuneable and persist across reboots.

Stage 02

Captive Portal

Browser-based setup flow served directly from the ESP32 — no app, no account. Wi-Fi onboarding, location search, sensitivity slider, colour picker, and a strip-length finder that walks through calibrating the specific install. The goal was to make the out-of-box experience feel closer to a Nest than a Raspberry Pi project.

Project 04 — Naval Architecture

Marine
Strut

Custom V-strut for the propulsion system of a 110 ft hybrid motor yacht. Designed during my co-op at Gregory C. Marshall Naval Architect — dimensioned to ABS standards, shaped for hydrodynamic efficiency, and drafted for the shipyard.

ClientGCMNA
Vessel110 ft Motor Yacht
StandardABS
Material316 Stainless
D / 01

Design & Analysis

CAD · CFD · ABS Compliance
Stage 01

Profile & Sizing

A V-strut with a rough NACA airfoil cross-section, dimensioned against ABS standards for a 3½" shaft. Minimum barrel thickness, minor/major arm thickness, and overall length all sized off the shaft diameter per spec.

I ran simple 2D fluid sims comparing an airfoil profile to a cylindrical one — the wake behaviour is dramatically cleaner on the airfoil, which matters directly for propeller vibration.

Front profile
Side profile
V-strut assembly
Stage 02

Fluid Behaviour

2D fluid sim of high-speed water curl passing over the section profile. Not a full CFD study — intended to illustrate the wake-shedding difference between shapes, and to inform the angle-of-attack decision.

Vortex shedding visualisation
Stage 03

The 42° Decision

The most interesting call on the project. ABS requires a minimum 40° angle between strut legs for load-path reasons. But there's a less-obvious upper bound too — if the struts are angled at 90° relative to each other, their vortices reach the 4-blade propeller simultaneously, doubling the vibration impulse on each rotation.

If one strut's vortex was going to strike a propeller, the other strut's vortex would also collide with another propeller blade. This effectively doubles the force being applied during any given vortex strike. — Challenge Report, Dec 2023

42° threads that needle — comfortably above the 40° minimum, nowhere near the 90° failure mode, and with enough base separation to avoid a high-pressure zone between the two strut feet.

Stage 04

Bolt Pattern

Eight M24 316-stainless bolts per pad, sixteen per strut. Sizing approach: treat the bolt pattern as a beam section and match its section modulus to the strut's. The final pattern sits about 4% over the strut's capacity — enough margin without over-building.

Pads are bonded to the hull tunnels with Chockfast Orange — a two-component compound rated for marine service by ABS, Lloyd's, Bureau Veritas, DNV, and Germanischer Lloyd. Fibreglass fairing on top, sanded smooth, painted to match.

Project 05 — Tooling

Bahtinov
Mask

A parametric Bahtinov mask generator for astrophotography focusing. Punch in your telescope's aperture and focal length, get a printable SVG and a matching 3D model. Useful when your kit doesn't come with one and you'd rather not buy a $50 piece of plastic.

RolePersonal project
OutputSVG · STL
E / 01

Generator

SVG · CAD · Print
Stage 01

Pattern & Model

The slit geometry follows the standard Bahtinov derivation — three slit groups at specific angles, sized so the diffraction spikes converge on a focused star and split when you're off. From the generated SVG I extrude a mask body with a friction-fit lip for common telescope apertures.
