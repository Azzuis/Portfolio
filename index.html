Stage 01

Firmware

ESP32-C3 Arduino firmware polling Open-Meteo for rain probability on a configurable schedule and translating the forecast into colour and animation on a WS2811 strip. Sensitivity threshold, colour presets, and strip length are all user tuneable and persist across reboots.

Stage 02

Captive Portal

Browser-based setup flow served directly from the ESP32. No app, no account. Wi-Fi onboarding, location search, sensitivity slider, colour picker, and a strip-length finder that walks through calibrating the specific install. The goal was to make the out-of-box experience feel closer to a Nest than a Raspberry Pi project.

Stage 01

Puck and Dock

Two boards, one system, one app on your phone telling the whole thing what to do. The puck runs an nRF52832 with a single SK6812MINI-E, MCP73831 LiPo charger, and exposed pads for magnetic dock contacts. The dock handles pairing and power. BLE antenna keepout and charger routing were the two spots I sweated most.

Behaviour, meaning colour, animation, and which pucks do what, is set from a companion phone app over BLE. That keeps the pucks dumb and cheap, and puts all the configuration where users already know how to configure things.

Garbage
Remindr

A third member of the Remindr family that tells you which bin to put out this week. Calendar-aware, glanceable, silent until it matters. This one is a deliberate architectural departure: battery powered, with all internal logic running on an FPGA written in VHDL.

RoleConcept + ECAD
LogicFPGA / VHDL
PowerBattery
StatusWork in Progress
C / 01

Concept and ECAD

Form, Schematic, FPGA Logic
Stage 01

Early Work

Still in the sketch and schematic phase. This one is a work in progress, and it's deliberately a very different beast from Rain Remindr. No Wi-Fi, no ESP32, no wall power. The Garbage Remindr runs on a battery and uses an FPGA, with all internal logic written in VHDL, to handle the schedule state machine, display drive, and power management.

Why go this route? Three reasons. The schedule logic is naturally parallel and fits an FPGA better than a polling MCU. Battery life is the hard constraint, and an FPGA can sleep deeper and wake faster than a typical microcontroller stack. And from a portfolio standpoint, keeping the Remindr family architecturally varied means each product teaches me something different.

Stage 01

Drawing

A single-line diagram is the architectural plan of an electrical system. One line stands in for the full set of conductors, and everything important (the service entrance, breakers, transfer switch, panels, and branch feeds) gets represented with standard symbols. It's the first drawing any electrician, inspector, or facility manager looks at to understand how a building is powered.

This one is a mockup for a small community centre. Utility service at the top, main disconnect, automatic transfer switch feeding a backup generator, then branch distribution to the panels that serve the rest of the building. Standard stuff, drawn to the conventions you'd expect on a stamped set.

Stage 02

The 42 Degree Decision

The most interesting call on the project. ABS requires a minimum 40 degree angle between strut legs for load-path reasons. There's a less-obvious upper bound too. If the struts are angled at 90 degrees relative to each other, their vortices reach the 4-blade propeller simultaneously, doubling the vibration impulse on each rotation.

If one strut's vortex was going to strike a propeller, the other strut's vortex would also collide with another propeller blade. This effectively doubles the force being applied during any given vortex strike. Challenge Report, Dec 2023

42 degrees threads that needle. Comfortably above the 40 degree minimum, nowhere near the 90 degree failure mode, and with enough base separation to avoid a high-pressure zone between the two strut feet.

Project 06, Tooling

Bahtinov
Mask

A parametric Bahtinov mask generator for astrophotography focusing. Punch in your telescope's aperture and focal length, get a printable SVG and a matching 3D model.

RolePersonal project
OutputSVG, STL
F / 01

Generator

SVG, CAD, Print
Stage 01

What It Is

A Bahtinov mask is a focusing aid for astrophotography. You clip it over the front of a telescope, point at a bright star, and the slit pattern produces three diffraction spikes. When the star is in focus, the spikes form a symmetric X with the centre spike bisecting the other two. When it's out of focus, the centre spike shifts sideways. You adjust focus until it re-centres. Much faster and far more reliable than eyeballing focus through a stretched histogram.

The mask geometry follows the standard Bahtinov derivation: three slit groups at specific angles, sized so the diffraction spikes converge on a focused star and split when you're off. My generator takes aperture and focal length as inputs and spits out a printable SVG plus a matching 3D model with a friction-fit lip for common telescope sizes. Useful when your kit doesn't come with one and you'd rather not buy a 50 dollar piece of plastic.
